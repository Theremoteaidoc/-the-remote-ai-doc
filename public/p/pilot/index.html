The platform

One clinical engine. A family of surfaces.

Every surface reads the same crew record and the same vessel medical chest, so a case opened by an officer, reviewed by a physician, and documented in the clinic is always one case, not three systems sharing a name.

Officer triage

A guided front door on the vessel. The officer opens a case in seconds, nothing clinical asked of them, and it reaches a physician fast.

Shore physician

The case arrives with the crew member's history in view. Decision support checks prescribing against the vessel's own chest, then closes as one audited record.

Onboard clinic

A full clinical record for larger vessels: structured exams, prescribing and dispensing from the chest, admissions, sign-off, public-health reporting.

PEME review

Pre-employment exams from your clinics, digitized on upload and read as a trend, not a document. The engine flags; the physician always decides.

Crew wellbeing

A seafarer's own health in their pocket, in their language, offline-capable: check-ins, screeners, an urgent help path, and vitals with trends.

Fleet oversight

Dashboards, cohort analytics, and per-clinic scorecards across every vessel, so a medical director acts on a pattern, not one file at a time.

Prevention

The cases you never see are the ones that cost the most.

A club or owner typically has no visibility into a medical case until it crosses the deductible and becomes a claim. Below that line, on every vessel, health is building quietly, and the cheap window to intervene closes months before anyone ashore is watching.

Above the line
Visible. Reported. Reserved. Reviewed by the club.
≈ USD 8,000–10,000 deductible
Below the line
Rising blood pressure across two exams. Creeping blood sugar. Early cardiac risk. Building silently, seen by no one on shore.

SeaScope makes the below-the-line signals visible: routine vitals, screenings, and PEME findings tracked over time, so a crew member trending toward a preventable crisis is flagged while it is still cheap to fix.

Why it holds up

Built to be relied on, and to be inspected.

Safety is not a policy document bolted on afterward. It is enforced in the code path itself, on every surface, for every case.

Human decides, always

Decision support reasons through the case and checks the formulary and stock, but the clinician holds the final judgment. The machine flags; it never diagnoses.

Safe by construction

Severity floors cannot be silently downgraded. Risk scores are calculated, not estimated. Unsafe prescribing is blocked in code, even when asked for.

Auditable end to end

Every step from case opening to sign-off is signed, timestamped, and preserved, with role-scoped access. A record a P&I club or a flag-state inspector can rely on.
